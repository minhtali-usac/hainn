Black Hole Scaling Relations and Their Evolution

Overview

Black hole mass correlates remarkably well with the velocity dispersion, luminosity and stellar mass of the host spheroid, and these relations are the workhorse used to estimate black hole masses in the vast majority of galaxies and to calibrate feedback models. Their reliability at the extremes is much less certain. At the low-mass end, dwarf galaxies and nucleated systems may deviate from the local relations or show a much larger intrinsic scatter, and dynamical measurements there are heavily affected by resolution-limited selection effects. At the high-mass end, the most massive galaxies host black holes that appear to lie above the standard relations, hinting at a fundamental break associated with dry-merger driven assembly. Because different mass estimators are calibrated against each other, systematic biases can propagate through the entire framework, so cross-calibration between independent methods is as important as extending the dynamic range.

My Research

I contribute homogeneous dynamical masses at both extremes of the relations. I am a member of the team on an accepted Hubble Space Telescope Cycle 34 programme (GO 18383) designed to test a fundamental break in the black hole–galaxy scaling relations for the most massive black holes, and on an accepted ALMA Cycle 13 programme (2026.1.00913.S) that cross-calibrates black hole mass methods in Seyfert nuclei using atomic [C I](1–0) gas dynamics. My simulation work quantifies the selection biases that spatial resolution imposes on such samples, which is a prerequisite for interpreting any measured scatter or break as physical.
