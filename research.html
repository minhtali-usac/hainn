Research

Black Holes & Their Host Galaxies

I measure the masses of central black holes dynamically, from the motions of stars and cold gas deep inside galactic nuclei, and I span the full mass range from intermediate-mass black holes in dwarf galaxies and star clusters to the ultramassive black holes of giant ellipticals. Measured this way, nearby galaxies become fossil records of how black holes were seeded and how they grew with their hosts.

03Scaling Relations

Black Hole and Galaxy Coevolution

Black holes and their host galaxies appear to grow together. I test how tightly that coupling holds across galaxy type, mass and environment, using dynamical masses paired with detailed measurements of the hosts.

Approach

One Measurement Chain, Many Tracers

All four themes rest on the same measurement chain. I start from spatially resolved observations of a galactic nucleus, extract the motions of stars or cold gas, and fit dynamical models in which the black hole mass is one free parameter alongside the stellar mass-to-light ratio and the disk inclination.

Using several tracers on the same galaxies (stellar kinematics from JWST/NIRSpec and ground-based IFS, cold molecular and atomic gas from ALMA) lets me cross-calibrate methods and expose systematic differences that single-method samples cannot reveal.
