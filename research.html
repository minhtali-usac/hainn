Black Holes &
Their Cosmic Hosts

We measure central black‑hole masses across the full mass spectrum — from intermediate‑mass systems to ultramassive black holes — combining high‑resolution stellar and molecular‑gas kinematics to constrain formation channels and co‑evolutionary pathways over cosmic time.

Credit: NASA/ESA HST
01

Intermediate-Mass Black Holes in Nuclear Star Clusters

Nuclear star clusters (NSCs) are among the densest stellar systems known and sometimes coexist with central black holes. We combine end‑to‑end ELT/HARMONI simulations with dynamical modelling of resolved stellar kinematics to measure black‑hole masses and discriminate NSC formation channels (in‑situ star formation versus cluster inspiral).

Massive Black Holes in Dwarf and Low-Mass Galaxies

We extend dynamical black‑hole mass measurements into the dwarf and low‑mass regime using ELT stellar kinematics and ALMA molecular‑gas dynamics, where the sphere of influence subtends only a few tens of milliarcseconds.

Ultramassive Black Holes and the High-Mass End of the Scaling Relations

At the highest galaxy masses we test for departures from canonical MBH–σ and MBH–L relations, investigating whether ultramassive black holes arise from dry‑merger driven assembly or alternate growth pathways.
